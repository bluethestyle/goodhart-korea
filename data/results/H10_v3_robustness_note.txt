(Robustness replication with approximate CPI: r = -0.727; qualitative results unchanged.)

1. Does r = -0.856 survive rank-based correlation measures?
   Yes — with identical sign. Spearman rho = -0.833 and Kendall tau = -0.714
   for the same CPI-residual first-differenced series. All three correlation families
   agree on a strong-to-moderate negative association between social welfare spending
   amplitude growth and wealth Gini growth. Rank-based measures are immune to outlier
   leverage and distributional assumptions, so sign agreement across Pearson/Spearman/
   Kendall constitutes meaningful triangulation. The 95% bootstrap CIs are:
     Pearson   [-0.988, -0.153]
     Spearman  [-1.000, -0.240]
   Both exclude zero. The upper bound approaching zero from below is expected at n = 8
   and indicates uncertainty about magnitude, not about sign direction. Across all
   15 field-outcome pairs in H10 v3, 14/15 show Pearson/
   Spearman/Kendall sign consistency, confirming social welfare is not an outlier in
   sign stability.

2. Leave-one-out (LOO) stability.
   LOO removes one of the 8 observations and recomputes Pearson r. Sign is preserved
   in ALL 8 iterations. The worst-case removal
   is year 2025, yielding r = -0.539 (25.9%
   magnitude change from baseline). LOO range: [-0.952, -0.539].
   This confirms no single leverage point drives the result. Period-split analysis:
   pre-2020 subsample r = n/a (n<3) (n<3, uninformative), post-2020 r = -0.708 (n=6).
   Rolling 5-year windows: 4/4 windows preserve the negative sign.

3. Honest assessment of n = 8.
   Wealth Gini is available 2017–2025 (9 annual observations); first differencing
   reduces usable pairs to n = 8. This is genuinely small. The two-tailed p-value
   for |r| ~ 0.86 at n = 8 is approximately 0.007 (well below alpha = 0.05),
   but standard power analysis yields ~70% power to detect a true rho = 0.70 at
   alpha = 0.05 — the test is not severely underpowered for a large true effect, but
   would miss moderate-sized effects (~0.50) ~30% of the time. No alternative welfare
   outcome series (poverty rate, NHIS payouts, NPS recipients) is available in the
   offline data repository — this is a confirmed data limitation, not a selection
   artefact. The nearest available internal series (gini_period, budget execution
   concentration) is NOT a social welfare outcome.

4. Paper recommendation.
   H5 should retain its main estimate but carry an explicit small-sample caveat.
   Recommended language: "The social welfare field shows a consistently negative
   CPI-residual correlation (Pearson r = -0.86, Spearman rho = -0.83,
   Kendall tau = -0.71, n = 8 annual pairs, 95% CI [-0.99, -0.15])
   between spending-amplitude growth and wealth-Gini growth. Leave-one-out analysis
   confirms sign stability across all 8 sub-samples; however, given
   n = 8 the magnitude estimate carries substantial uncertainty and should be
   interpreted as a directional indicator. Replication with longer Gini series or
   administrative welfare outcome data (poverty headcount, NHIS payouts) is warranted."
   H5 need not be demoted to speculative, but weakened confidence language is warranted.
